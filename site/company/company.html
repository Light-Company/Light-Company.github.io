The space beyond the screen.

Much of computing lives in a rectangle. Physical work happens elsewhere: on a bench, beside a machine, around a kitchen table, in the space a hand is about to reach.

Light can meet that work where it is. A projected outline can tell a person where to place an object. A camera can help determine whether it arrived. Together, they make an interface that shares the surface with the task.

That is the starting point for Prism. We want intelligence to become easier to see, question and use in the physical world.

One idea, two places to begin.

Prism Robotics

Robot evaluation needs a physical setup that can be described, checked and revisited. Prism Robotics brings projected staging and camera-based verification to that work, with a broader direction toward a shared history of scenes and results.

Prism IRL

Prism IRL explores information and interaction on everyday surfaces. The interface comes into the room: guidance where the hands are, information where the object is, light that helps a person act.

What we want the work to feel like.

Legible. A person should understand what the system is asking, what it saw and what it still does not know.

Grounded. Real footage, real materials and real operating constraints should shape the product. The workbench gets a vote.

Open to correction. A failed trial, an uncertain observation or a disagreement is part of the record. Good tools make those moments easier to examine.

Generous to the next person. Knowledge should survive a shift change. An experiment should be understandable to someone who was not there.

Build the next layer with us.

This work sits across optics, perception, robotics, software and interaction design. It rewards people who can move between a careful explanation and a stubborn physical problem.
