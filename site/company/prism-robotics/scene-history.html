Save the condition, not just a picture.

A photograph can remind you what a table looked like. A useful scene record should also explain what mattered: the objects, target arrangement, calibration, task, policy and acceptance rules. It should distinguish what the system observed from what a person intended.

Our proposed scene history gives that context a stable identity. A trial refers back to a scene revision, so the result does not become detached from its starting point when a video moves folders or someone renames a spreadsheet.

Change one thing. Keep the question clear.

Imagine branching from a known scene to test a new object position. The baseline remains available. The branch makes the intended difference explicit, and the comparison asks a specific question: did this policy still succeed when the cup moved?

Position, orientation, clutter, objects and lighting are useful experimental axes. They do not all have the same physical controls. A future workflow should make both the intended change and the limits of its verification visible.

A difference you can point to.

The proposed comparison view brings two conditions and their episodes together. The reviewer sees what changed in the setup, which policy ran and what evidence supports the result. A changed rubric or camera configuration should be visible alongside a changed object position.

The goal is a conversation with fewer missing pieces: “this is the scene,” “this is the change,” “this is what we observed.”

Return to the question that mattered.

A physical replay would take a saved condition and project the staging instructions again. An operator restores the objects. The station checks what it can observe. The next trial gets its own record, linked to the earlier condition.

Replay is a reconstruction, not time travel. The table may be the same while friction, wear, exposure or a deformable object differs. The product should help the team see those boundaries.

The lab's memory, shared.

Beyond individual scene records, we are exploring collaborative review, named test suites, scheduled regression runs and a history of why a result was accepted or questioned. These are product directions shown here to make the ambition concrete; this page does not announce their availability.

The measure of success is human: a new researcher should be able to understand an old failure without finding the one person who was in the room.
